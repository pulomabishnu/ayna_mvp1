## Where we are (bundled catalog, Nov 2)

- Ready to recommend: **64 of 281 (23%)**. Research in progress 163, clinician review 16, browse only 38.
- What blocks the most: **allergen info (125 products)**, interaction info (48), breastfeeding info (4), pregnancy info (3).
- Hold rate by group: regular 0.34, trying 0.56, post-menopause 0.59, pregnant 0.73, on medicines 0.84, breastfeeding 0.86, allergic 0.97.
- Strong matches are now rare (2 for the regular persona). That is honest: few answers and products reach high confidence yet.
- Safety audit: no violations across 281 products x 8 personas.

The gate is not the problem. The data is: full ingredient and allergen lists
for 125 products would cut most holds. That is team work with sources (CLAUDE.md
catalog rules), not something to loosen in code.

## Research notes

- **Abstention.** Holding back only improves decisions when higher confidence
  really means higher accuracy (no "inversion zones"). Check this on outcome
  data per band before trusting confidence for more than holds ([Confidence Gate
  Theorem, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09947)). Our calibration report
  already flags `inverted` bands.
- **Calibrated lists.** Steck's re-ranker matches a list's mix to the person's
  needs (KL divergence, greedy, (1-1/e) guarantee); newer work personalizes the
  trade-off and calibrates several goals at once ([survey, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02643v1)).
  Our minPerNeed fill and per-brand cap do a simple version; a proper KL term is next.
- **Fairness.** Evaluate per group, not only overall ([fairness and diversity
  survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04644)). `match:groups` is the start.
- **Interactions.** St John's wort makes hormonal contraceptives, including
  implants, less effective. Don't combine them ([MHRA Drug Safety Update](https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/st-john-s-wort-interaction-with-hormonal-contraceptives-including-implants)).
  It's in our interaction table and now covered by a synthetic test.
- **Regulation.** FDA's Jan 2026 general wellness update allows wellness outputs
  and "see a clinician" suggestions, but not outputs called abnormal, naming a
  disease, using clinical thresholds, recommending treatment or giving ongoing
  alerts to manage a disease. Being "wellness" does not make a product safe or
  effective ([Honigman summary](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3236)). Keep
  match copy as fit to what she told us, never a treatment recommendation.
  Have counsel review before any learned model or wearable-based alert ships.

# Matching engine research, Oct 29 2026 (P0 step 2)

What large recommenders and the safety literature do, and what ayna takes from
each. Sources were read directly; summaries are ours.

## 1. Architecture: gate → candidates → rank → explain

- **YouTube** (Covington et al., RecSys 2016) splits recommendation into a
  candidate-generation model that narrows millions of items to hundreds, and a
  separate ranking model that orders them.
  [research.google/pubs/pub45530](https://research.google/pubs/pub45530?hl=de)
- **ayna:** health products need one more stage *before* candidates: a hard,
  deterministic eligibility gate. Ranking may never re-admit what the gate
  removed, and fallbacks are candidates like any other (they go through the
  gate). This is `recommendationEligibilityForUser` (`src/matching/gate.js`).

## 2. Calibration (two different meanings)

- **List calibration** (Steck, RecSys 2018; survey arXiv 2507.02643): the mix
  of a list should match the mix of a person's interests, measured by KL
  divergence, traded off against relevance with λ and built greedily.
  [arxiv.org/html/2507.02643v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02643v1)
- **Spotify** (2025) learns the right content-type mix per context with a
  contextual bandit (1.5% ε-greedy, exploration sampled around her history),
  then builds slates greedily balancing relevance against KL calibration.
  Online: +36.6% podcast impression-to-stream, +3.93% overall.
  [arxiv.org/html/2509.05460v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05460v1)
- **Score calibration** (Guo et al., ICML 2017): modern models are
  over-confident; a one-parameter temperature scaling fixes most of it.
  [proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)
- **ayna:** the ecosystem's per-need coverage already does list calibration
  across her needs (weights by need order). The Match % is *not* a
  probability and must not be read as one (P1: label "Fit", not "chance it
  works"). We have no outcome data to calibrate against yet, so the honest
  move now is fewer, coarser bands that agree everywhere (one Strong threshold
  for band and label) and abstaining when inputs are missing. Real
  calibration (reliability curves of "Strong" vs. reported "helped") is a P3
  item once outcomes are collected.

## 3. Abstention (reject option)

- Machine learning with a reject option (Hendrickx et al., survey,
  arXiv 2107.11277): models should abstain when likely wrong, which matters
  most in decision support where errors are costly; rejection splits into
  ambiguity (conflicting evidence) and novelty (outside what the model knows).
  [arxiv.org/abs/2107.11277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11277)
- **ayna mapping:**
  - *novelty* → we lack a product fact ("Checking this product") or a
    profile fact ("We need one answer");
  - *ambiguity* → her answers conflict or are old ("Please confirm");
  - *expert-required* → clinician review open ("Match being reviewed").
  In every case: no %, a reason, a next step. Unknown is never safe.

## 4. Regulation (US, Jan 2026)

- **CDS guidance** (FDA, Jan 6 2026, supersedes 2022): non-device CDS must
  let the *clinician* independently review the basis for a recommendation;
  it covers HCP-facing tools only and gives no new policy for consumer-facing
  decision support. [Covington summary](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance)
- **General wellness guidance** (Jan 6 2026): non-invasive sensors may
  estimate values like HRV under enforcement discretion if they don't
  reference diseases or thresholds, don't prompt clinical actions, and only
  suggest seeing a clinician without naming a condition.
  [King & Spalding summary](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents)
- **ayna:** ayna is consumer-facing, so neither safe harbour clearly applies.
  Stay in product-discovery territory: no diagnoses, no treatment decisions,
  wearable data never creates a need or a threshold-based alert (already true:
  `src/health/policy.js`), every reason shown must come from the decision
  trace so the basis is reviewable, and the review queue gives a human
  clinician the last word on higher-risk products. Not legal advice: have
  counsel confirm before marketing copy changes.

## 5. Decisions for P0

1. One gate function, called by `evaluateSafety` (so every surface) and by the
   ecosystem fallback.
2. Life stage only from her answers; scoped products need an explicit stage.
3. Unknown safety facts hold, by route (systemic vs. topical vs. none).
4. Per-fact freshness; stale safety answers hold and ask.
5. Field-based product completeness, plus a clinician review queue table.
6. Analytics see event names and product ids only; never needs, stages,
   medicines, allergies or the hold reason text.
